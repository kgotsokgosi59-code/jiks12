# Licensing music for the Jiks Academy site

Plain guide to what is actually needed before an MP3 can be used as background
music on the site. **Not legal advice** — but it will save you a phone call.

---

## The short version

Buying a track (iTunes, Bandcamp, a streaming download) lets **you** listen to it.
It does **not** let you play it on a website. Playing music on a site is a public
use, and it needs permission from the rights holders.

## What you need for one track

| Right | Who usually owns it | What it covers |
|---|---|---|
| **Master use** | The record label (or the artist, if self-released) | The actual recording — the MP3 itself |
| **Publishing / sync** | The composers' music publisher | The song: melody, lyrics, composition |

Both are required for a commercial site. A **royalty-free library** subscription
(Artlist, Epidemic Sound, Musicbed, AudioNetwork, Pond5, and similar) bundles
both into one licence — this is by far the simplest route, and most of them
explicitly cover "website background music". Check the word **web** is in the
licence; some cheaper tiers cover social video only.

## South Africa specifically

If you clear South African music directly or host it yourself, expect these
collecting societies to be involved:

- **SAMRO** — composers' and publishers' performing rights
- **CAPASSO** — mechanical and digital rights for composers and publishers
- **SAMPRA** — needletime for recording artists and labels

A blanket licence from these bodies can cover public use of repertoire in their
catalogues. Ask them directly what a **website / background music** tariff costs
for a site of your size — it is usually far cheaper than people fear.

## The safest options, in order

1. **Keep the Spotify embed.** Spotify already licenses the stream; embedding
   their official player is permitted. This is on the About page now.
2. **Commission a local artist.** Pay an East Rand producer for an exclusive
   instrumental, get a short written assignment, and you own a bespoke soundtrack
   that fits the brand. One-page deal, no renewals, supports the scene.
3. **Royalty-free library.** Fast, cheap, and covers both rights. Download the
   licence certificate and keep it in the evidence folder.
4. **Clear the commercial tracks directly.** Most expensive, slowest, and the
   label can simply say no.

## Clearing a Kabza De Small track directly

You would need two yeses: the **label** holding the master, and the **publisher**
holding the song. Send the request below to both. Expect a fee quoted per year,
per territory, and expect it to be quoted for a "corporate website" tariff.

## Permission request — email template

> Subject: Licence request — background music on jiks-academy (small business site)
>
> Hello,
>
> We run Jiks Academy, a small creative house in Daveyton, East Rand. We would
> like to use **"[TRACK]"** by **[ARTIST]** as optional background music on our
> website. It would play only when a visitor presses play, and it is not
> synchronised to video or advertising.
>
> Could you tell us:
> 1. Whether you can licence **master use** (or who to ask, if that sits elsewhere),
> 2. Which publisher we should approach for the **composition** rights,
> 3. Your fee for **web background use**, worldwide, for one year, and
> 4. Any credit line you require on the site.
>
> For scale: we are a two-movement community project, not a broadcaster. If there
> is an emerging-artist or small-business rate, we would be grateful for it.
>
> Thank you,
> Jiks Academy — kgotsokgosi59@gmail.com — +27 67 702 4063

## House rule

A track goes on the site when its row in the register says `cleared` **and** the
invoice or certificate is saved next to it. Not before.
